Some trips go exactly as planned. This wasn't one of them.

After spending a month in Hanoi, I was ready to get out of the city and finally do something I'd been thinking about for years: take a proper motorbike trip through Vietnam.

The plan was to ride from Hanoi to Cát Bà Island, explore some historical sites along the way, catch an evening ferry, spend Sunday riding around the island, and return to Hanoi on Monday morning.

Simple enough, right?

Well, I underestimated the distances, overestimated how much I could fit into one day, and somehow managed to miss the last ferry. But let's start from the beginning.

## Getting the Bike: From Honda Wave to Yamaha PG-1

I'd been getting around Hanoi on a rented Honda Wave, including a 90 km round trip to Thám Lắm. It had served me well, but for a longer journey with several stops, I wanted something a little more comfortable.

That's where the Yamaha PG-1 came in.

I rented it for 400,000 VND for two days, and honestly, it was worth every dong. Compared to the Wave, it felt bigger, more comfortable, surprisingly quick, and easy on fuel. The semi-automatic gearbox is also great if you're not particularly experienced with manual motorcycles.

I rented from the same shop in Hanoi:

[Motorbike rental location – Google Maps](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Lw1hYuxGR1i64gXV9)

They allowed me to pick it up on Friday at 5:30 PM and return it on Monday at 9 AM, which meant I could make the most of the weekend.

My original idea was to leave Friday evening, but some Vietnamese friends quickly convinced me otherwise. Riding on unlit highways with unpredictable traffic at night wasn't exactly the best way to start my first proper road trip.

So I decided to get some sleep and leave early Saturday morning.

## Day 1: An Ambitious Itinerary

The alarm went off at 5:30 AM.

The night before, I'd spent quite a bit of time bookmarking places along the route: Chùa Phật Tích, Kiếp Bạc Temple, Côn Sơn Temple, Yên Tử Mountain, and a few others.

Looking at the map, it seemed perfectly manageable. In reality, I had absolutely no idea how much time I'd need at each place.

The plan was to visit as many spots as possible, reach the ferry terminal in the evening, and make it to Cát Bà before bedtime.

First, though, I needed fuel and coffee.

### Fuel, Coffee and the Road Out of Hanoi

The rental shop had transferred the remaining fuel from my old Honda Wave into the PG-1, so I only needed a quick 35,000 VND top-up to fill the tank.

Next came coffee. I found one of the few places open that early ([coffee shop location](https://maps.app.goo.gl/rMDBsALcBkyWPsh89?g_st=ic)) and paid 60,000 VND for a rather mediocre cup.

Not the best coffee I've ever had, but caffeine was caffeine.

Then I was on the road.

The roads outside Hanoi were quite bumpy, with potholes appearing when you least expected them. At a few points, I was actually glad I wasn't riding a bigger motorcycle. Some of those holes would have made for a pretty unpleasant surprise.

### Stop 1: Chùa Phật Tích and the 250,000 VND Sausage Problem

My first stop was Chùa Phật Tích, a Buddhist temple complex in the countryside.

Even if you're not particularly interested in religious sites, it's worth visiting for the walk up and the views of the surrounding area.

  src={phatTich}

After making my way to the top, I realised I'd made my first rookie mistake of the trip: I had no small cash and desperately needed water.

Going back down to find an ATM or somewhere to exchange money would have meant another 40-minute round trip.

My solution? Buy 250,000 VND worth of snacks from a local vendor.

That got me three sausages, two bottles of water and a fresh coconut. She got a much bigger sale than she probably expected, and I finally had some smaller bills.

The sausages weren't really my thing, so I ended up giving them to some of the other vendors on my way down.

Everyone seemed happy with the arrangement.

### Stop 2: Kiếp Bạc Temple and My First Drone Flight

Next was Kiếp Bạc Temple.

The ride took around 50 minutes, and I really enjoyed this part of the journey. The roads were scenic, traffic was manageable, and I was finally getting into the rhythm of travelling by motorcycle.

I paid 10,000 VND for parking and grabbed a 40,000 VND bowl of phở before exploring the grounds.

But I had another reason to be excited about this stop.

I'd recently bought a DJI Mini 2 SE, and this was its first proper trip with me. I'd already seen some aerial footage of the area online, so I was eager to see what I could capture myself.

After finding a suitable spot, I launched the drone.

For a beginner, I was pretty happy with the results. It was also a nice change to see the places I'd been riding through from above.

[Watch the drone footage on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df115iyS93j/).

### Stop 3: Côn Sơn Temple and the Mountain Trek I Didn't Sign Up For

My next destination was Chùa Côn Sơn.

The temple complex was peaceful and interesting to explore.

<Figure
  src={conSon}
  alt="Looking down through pine branches over Côn Sơn: a yellow-tiled roof in the foreground, red-tiled temple halls and a courtyard below, and forested hills beyond."
  caption="Côn Sơn from above."
/>
While walking around, I noticed a set of stone stairs leading into the forest.

I assumed they would take me to a small shrine or another viewpoint nearby. So naturally, I started climbing.

Turns out, it wasn't quite the short walk I had imagined.

What followed was roughly an hour of continuous uphill trekking through the mountain. By the time I reached the top, I was exhausted. I stopped for a 40,000 VND coconut water along the way, which was very much needed.

Still, the view at the top made the climb worthwhile. You could see the surrounding landscape stretching out below.

After making my way back down, I also took the drone out again to capture some aerial footage of the temple complex.

At this point, I was already behind schedule, but I still had one more major destination in mind.

### Stop 4: Yên Tử Mountain – A Serious Miscalculation

It was mid-afternoon, and I was starting to realise that my itinerary might have been a little too ambitious.

I should probably have skipped another stop and headed towards Cát Bà. But a Vietnamese friend had strongly recommended Yên Tử, and I didn't want to miss it.

After a long ride, I arrived at around 2:30 PM.

I hadn't done much research beforehand, and that became obvious pretty quickly.

Yên Tử isn't just another temple you stop at for 30 minutes. It's an enormous historical and religious complex spread across the mountain.

<Figure
  src={yenTuGate}
  alt="Looking out from a dark hall through a tall arched doorway: a paved path leads to a stone staircase and a gate, with a forested mountain behind."
  caption="One of the gates at Yên Tử, with the mountain still ahead."
/>

I bought a 520,000 VND ticket that included two cable car rides to the summit and a traditional meal. It sounded like a pretty good deal.

The problem was that even with the cable cars, properly exploring the area and reaching the peak could take four to five hours.

I also struggled to navigate the complex. There wasn't much English signage, and finding someone who could explain where everything was wasn't easy.

I spent around an hour wandering around, trying to find the restaurant included in my ticket.

<Figure
  src={yenTuHall}
  alt="A long, dim temple hall with rows of dark wooden chairs on either side of a polished aisle, leading to an altar with three golden seated Buddha figures."
  caption="Inside one of the halls at Yên Tử."
/>

By this point, I was hungry, tired and running out of daylight. I eventually gave up on exploring everything and pushed towards the iconic Lady Buddha statue near the summit.

The views were worth the effort, but I was beginning to realise just how badly I'd miscalculated the day.

<Figure
  src={yenTuSummit}
  alt="A large Buddhist flag flies over stone steps near the top of Yên Tử, with hazy mountain ridges below and a few people walking down."
  caption="Near the top of Yên Tử, just before 5 PM."

One important thing to know: Yên Tử deserves a full day. If you decide to visit, don't make the same mistake I did and squeeze it between several other destinations.

Also, drones aren't allowed at the summit, so keep that in mind if you're travelling with one.

## The Twist: I Missed the Last Ferry to Cát Bà

By the time I left Yên Tử, it was getting dark.

I'd visited several major sites, climbed mountains, spent hours riding, and barely had time to rest. All I wanted now was to reach Cát Bà, check into my pre-booked hostel and get some sleep.

The ride into Haiphong was actually quite pleasant compared to the roads around Hanoi. But once I reached the city centre and started asking locals about the ferry, I got the answer I didn't want to hear.

**The last ferry to Cát Bà had already left at 6 PM.**

I was too late.

Suddenly, the entire plan had to change. My hostel was on the island, I had nowhere to sleep in Haiphong, and I wasn't particularly interested in spending a fortune on a last-minute hotel.

So I started looking for the cheapest accommodation I could find.

Eventually, I found a basic hostel for 150,000 VND. Nothing fancy, but it had a bed, a roof and everything I needed for the night.

Not exactly the evening I'd planned, but at least I wasn't sleeping on the motorcycle.

## Changing the Plan: A 3:30 AM Wake-Up Call

After checking in, I needed to figure out how to salvage the remaining trip.

The earliest ferry I found was scheduled for 5:30 AM from Gót Ferry Terminal, roughly an hour's ride from where I was staying.

I also didn't want to waste my limited time on Cát Bà figuring out activities, so I booked the Alibaba Bay Tour online for 650,000 VND.

[Alibaba Bay Tour location](https://maps.app.goo.gl/C68pDuDaERoAscjw7)

With the logistics sorted, there was only one thing left to do: eat.

I found a place serving beef hot pot and ordered a massive meal for 130,000 VND. After the day I'd had, I wasn't going to complain about the portion size.

Then I headed back to the hostel, set my alarm for 3:30 AM, and called it a night.

The original plan was gone, but the adventure wasn't over.
