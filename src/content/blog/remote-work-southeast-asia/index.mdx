I'm writing this from Hanoi, Vietnam, where I work remotely as a software developer. Outside working hours, I'm exploring Southeast Asia, figuring out where to go next, and trying to build a few things of my own.

It's a pretty good setup, but it has limits. A remote job gives me options I wouldn't otherwise have; it doesn't give me unlimited ones. I earn around $1,800 a month, which is not a developer's fortune, and I pay fees to move and spend my own money internationally because my banking options aren't exactly ideal. I'm not going to pretend I can live anywhere I want without thinking about the cost.

But I can live here. I can explore new places, eat well enough, get around, and have experiences I wouldn't get by staying home. I just have to be a little more deliberate about how I spend my money.

Sometimes I think we underestimate what's possible with what we already have. I'd like to find out how true that is for me: where an ordinary remote salary lets me live, what things cost in real life, and which experiences are worth the time and the money. This blog isn't about living a luxury lifestyle in Southeast Asia, and it isn't a guide to becoming a digital nomad in five easy steps.

## Why Southeast Asia?

This isn't my first time living abroad. I studied in Hungary and worked remotely there. Later, after returning to Azerbaijan, I found myself wanting to travel again.

My brother helped me get to Thailand during a difficult period in my life. We explored Phuket and Bangkok together, and when he went home, I continued to Da Nang on my own. I spent around two months there before returning to Azerbaijan.

On August 16, I came back to Southeast Asia to start this stretch of the journey.

I didn't have everything figured out. I still don't. But there are so many places I want to see, and having a remote job gives me a way to explore them without putting my career on hold. I want to see how far I can take that, both geographically and financially.

## What you'll find here

I'll write about travelling around Southeast Asia, the real cost of living in different places, motorcycles, remote work, and the projects I'm building along the way.

Some posts will be practical: spending breakdowns, comparisons between places, and ways to travel on a limited budget. Others will be about the experience itself: discovering somewhere new, trying something I've never done before, or finding out that an idea sounded considerably better before I actually tried it. And some will be about work: what it's like to do an ordinary job from somewhere that's still unfamiliar.

I'm also interested in building things. I'd like to create a second source of income rather than depend entirely on my salary, and I'll share that process too, including the experiments that don't work out.

I don't want this to be another travel blog where every place looks incredible, every decision works out, and apparently nobody ever has to deal with boring financial problems. I'd rather show the useful parts, the enjoyable parts, and the occasional ridiculous parts of trying to make this way of living work.

You don't need to be a software engineer to follow along. You might be planning your first trip to Southeast Asia, working remotely yourself, looking for ways to travel more cheaply, or simply curious about what life looks like outside your usual surroundings.

## Starting from Hanoi

I know that not everyone can work remotely, and I'm aware that having this opportunity is a privilege. But if you already have a remote job, you might have more freedom than you're using. You don't necessarily need a huge income, a perfect plan, or enough savings to disappear for a year. Your circumstances will decide what's realistic.

Mine, for now, are Hanoi, a job to do, and more places I want to see than my budget will cover. I don't know yet where I'll go next, or which of the things I'm building will turn into anything. I'll write it down as I find out, including what it costs and the parts that don't go to plan.

If any of that sounds interesting, you're welcome to follow along.
